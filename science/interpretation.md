# Physical meaning and resources

The [main theorem](memory_law.md) determines the smallest hidden quantum carrier needed by a physical emulator of one known, finite, static environment. The emulator must accept actual quantum inputs and reproduce the outputs and their correlations with inaccessible references under arbitrary allowed interventions. Classical numerical prediction of described experiments is a different task and is not excluded.

## What the bath decomposition separates

| Part | Operational role | Required simulator storage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sector label j | A conserved label selecting conditional dynamics | Classical storage |
| Active factor R_j | Can receive and later return arbitrary quantum information through port controls | Coherent storage of dimension r_j |
| Invisible multiplicity B_j | Its evolution cannot influence future port experiments | Can be traced out |

This decomposition and its controllability properties come from earlier quantum-control theory. General quantum/classical/irrelevant decompositions and quantum-memory cost with free classical records also have established antecedents. The contribution here is the sharp weighted uniform process-error law and its repeated operational witness. See [related work](../references/related_work.md).

## Why small sectors may be discarded but not partially compressed

At hard memory dimension D, one emulator can retain every sector with active dimension at most D, storing its label classically. Replacing all other sectors changes the complete process by at most their total probability.

For the converse, the laboratory first reads a sector label. In every oversized sector it repeatedly transfers one half of a Bell pair into the bath, erases all accessible quantum carriers and retrieves the payload. The reference stays sealed across the reset. A D-dimensional hidden memory can transmit Bell overlap at most D/r_j per round, even after conditioning on all earlier classical history. Repetition removes its residual passing probability. The exact uniform error is therefore the omitted sector weight.

The hard-cap convention matters. Neither an average quantum-memory entropy nor an average number of qubits is optimized. For hardware registers restricted to k qubits, substitute dimension 2 to the power k in the tail law.

## Resource contract

| Resource or promise | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Hidden persistent quantum systems | Every carrier surviving a laboratory reset counts jointly toward the hard dimension cap, on every branch. |
| Classical state and adaptation | Arbitrary classical storage, randomness and causal history are allowed. |
| Visible port | Its physical dimension is fixed and given; the laboratory can intervene on it. It is reset in the distinguishing experiment. |
| Working ancillas | Finite but unrestricted ideal laboratory systems may be used. Every accessible work system is erased at the designated memory cuts. |
| External reference | A fresh test reference remains inaccessible to the port/controller across its cut and is used only for the terminal Bell test. It cannot act as an uncounted bypass. |
| Controls and time | Full local control, arbitrary finite experiment duration and arbitrarily accurate reachable synthesis are allowed. Their cost is not optimized. |
| Model knowledge | The finite Hamiltonian and initial bath state are specified. The theorem is not a learning algorithm. |
| Quantum side channels | No additional persistent quantum program, controller, clock or remote carrier is free. Any such simulator path must count. |

## Consequence for classical noise models

The theorem excludes even invasive classical feedforward emulators when an occupied active factor has dimension greater than one. This is stronger than testing whether one fixed, noninvasive random-field description works. Under the stated all-time stabilized control family, exact general classical-memory emulation is possible precisely when every occupied active factor is scalar. In that case a classical common-cause realization already suffices.

This collapse is specific to the complete family of experiments generated by a static finite bath. General finite-time quantum processes with classical feedforward form a strictly larger class than mixtures of memoryless processes. A single chosen grid can conceal quantum memory even when another reveals it. The theorem does not collapse that general hierarchy.

## Limits with operational consequences

The result is a structural limit, not an efficient laboratory diagnostic. The [weak transverse-term example](examples.md) has maximal all-time classical-emulation error for every nonzero coupling but can be arbitrarily close to a classical process on a fixed time interval. Control complexity, a shortest witness time, robustness to imperfect calibration or gates, large-bath limits, and optimal finite-horizon memory remain outside the established account.

No communication-rate, computational speedup, thermodynamic saving or hardware advantage follows from the memory dimension alone. The scientific consequence is a precise answer to which coherent part of a static bath must survive in any universal physical emulator, and how much uniform process error is unavoidable when that carrier is too small.
